Today: the estate land survey, the joint forest–revenue survey, and a rebound in coffee prices.
Coffee Board land survey

The Coffee Board is running a detailed land survey of coffee-growing regions, including Karnataka. It is also assessing how drought and heavy rain have hit the crop. The state government says it will survey forest land and buffer zones too.

Source: The New Indian Express, 23.09.2026 (Coffee Board source, unnamed)
“About 30% of estates on encroached land”

An unnamed Coffee Board source told the paper that about 30% of Karnataka's estates sit on forest or revenue land not yet regularised — mostly in Kodagu, Chikkamagaluru, Shivamogga and Hassan. The same source said 30–35% of the coffee area would fall in protected areas if the ESA is notified as is. These are not official figures.

Source: The New Indian Express, 23.09.2026 (Coffee Board source, unnamed)
9.94 lakh ha
Land the state's expert committee lists as ‘deemed forest’ — land with forest characteristics, whoever owns it.
Mapping farms, homes and encroachments in forest

The joint survey begun last year will mark forest boundaries and identify homes, cultivation, encroachments and revenue grants inside forest land, said Rajender Kumar Kataria, Additional Chief Secretary, Revenue. The report says this data could help keep plantations and villages out of the ESA.

The survey itself should take about three months. With district and state committee review and Cabinet approval, the whole process may take six to eight months.

Source: Deccan Herald, 23.09.2026

Off the 3-month low

December New York arabica closed up 3.60 c/lb (1.32%). The report says funds covering short positions after a three-week sell-off drove the rise.

The day before (22.09) arabica had fallen 1.48% and robusta 2.31%.
