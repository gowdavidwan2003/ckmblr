Today's top story: the KPA warns El Niño could cut Karnataka's coffee crop, and an EU export rule deadline is drawing near.
15–20% fall
KPA estimate for how much Karnataka's 2026-27 coffee crop could drop, if El Niño heat and dry spells intensify.
UPASI and the Coffee Board see it differently

The Karnataka Planters' Association (KPA) says Karnataka grows about 70% of India's coffee. UPASI projects a smaller 7–10% fall across South India and the Northeast. The Coffee Board's post-blossom estimate puts India's 2026-27 crop at 4 lakh tonnes, up from last year's 3.7 lakh tonnes. UPASI expects Arabica alone to drop to about 1 lakh tonnes. Both associations say prices are unlikely to move much. KPA has asked the state and central governments to step in.

Source: Kodagu Express (quoting KPA, UPASI), 13.10.2026
EUDR takes effect 30 December

The EU's deforestation rule (EUDR) takes effect on 30 December 2026 for large and medium exporters of coffee, rubber and soy to Europe. Every shipment will need the geolocation of the land it was grown on, and proof no forest was cleared there after December 2020. Traceability firm TRST01 says its platform is ready and claims 20 Indian coffee exporters are already on it — a company figure, not independently confirmed.

Source: The Wire (ANI release, TRST01 company announcement), 13.10.2026
