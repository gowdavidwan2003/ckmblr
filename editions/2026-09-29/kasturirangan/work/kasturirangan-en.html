At a glance

Reassuring for growers

The report says plantations, farmland and settlements were kept out of the ESA line. Farming is not on the ban list. It praises Kodagu's coffee estates.

Worrying for growers

The line is drawn village by village. If 20% of a village is marked sensitive, the whole village is listed. The report admits satellite maps cannot tell under-canopy plantations such as cardamom apart from forest.

What to watch

The Centre's final notification. The rules that apply will be the notification's, not this 2013 report's.

Source: HLWG Report Vol. I, pp. 96, 99, 113

Banned inside the ESA

Mining, quarrying, sand mining

Fully banned. Existing mines to close within 5 years or when the lease ends, whichever is earlier.

Thermal power plants

Banned.

'Red' category industry

Highly polluting units banned. The 'orange' list includes food processing, so it is not fully banned.

Large buildings, townships

Buildings of 20,000 m² and above, and townships, are banned.

Plantations and farming are not on this ban list.
Source: HLWG Report Vol. I, recommendations 5–9 (p. xiv)

Not banned,
but tightly controlled

Hydropower

30% of the river's flow must run even in the dry season. At least 3 km between projects. No more than 50% of a river basin affected.

Other large projects

Need Category 'A' environment clearance from the Centre. Wind farms to come under clearance too.

Gram Sabha

Any new project in an ESA village needs the Gram Sabha's consent and NOC.

Within 10 km of the ESA

Projects that need environment clearance fall under the EIA 2006 rules.

Source: HLWG Report Vol. I, recommendations 6–15 (pp. xiv–xv)

What it says
about coffee estates

Under the Gadgil report, estates would in time have to be replaced with natural forest. This report calls that clearly unacceptable to the plantation owners.

Its position: removing plantations is not the answer; greener practices should be rewarded. It notes Kodagu's coffee areas carry high biodiversity.

It also names the problems: labour shortage, and no organic premium without expensive certification.

Source: HLWG Report Vol. I, pp. 99–102

What growers told
the committee

Views recorded in Volume II — not the report's conclusions.
Kodagu planters

Keep plantations out of the sensitive zone altogether.

Plantation sector submission

12.5 lakh growers and 14 lakh workers in tea, coffee, rubber and spices across the three southern states. Argued that compulsory organic would cut yields 30–40%.

Coffee Board (via Commerce Dept.)

Shade-grown coffee is eco-friendly. Stopping all chemicals would make coffee unviable. About 2,82,000 coffee farmers depend on it.

Source: HLWG Report Vol. II, PDF pp. 167, 172, 371–373

Money and incentives:
what it recommends

Organic and a brand

A scheme to reward organic growing and a premium Western Ghats brand. Coffee Board, Spices Board and APEDA to meet and clear the barriers.

Timber transit rules

Review and revise the rules on moving timber from private land.

Money straight to villages

'Plus payments' paid directly to ESA village communities, and payment for the water and other services forests provide.

Western Ghats programme

₹1,000 crore, with costs shared 90:10 by Centre and state.

These are recommendations only. The report does not say how much has been implemented.
Source: HLWG Report Vol. I, recommendations 20–28 · pp. 112–113
