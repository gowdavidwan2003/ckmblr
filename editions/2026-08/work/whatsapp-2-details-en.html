1. Where prices stood

  • ICO composite (I-CIP) 287.29 US cents/lb — 287.26 in July, so effectively unchanged. Range for the month 279.38 to 301.97.
  • Colombian Milds 387.46 (+1.1%), Other Milds 361.31 (+0.7%), Brazilian Naturals 322.24 (+0.5%), Robustas 180.63 (−2.2%).
  • New York Arabica futures +0.9% (313.20); London Robusta −3.0% (167.63). The gap between the two markets widened 5.8% to 145.56.
  • Volatility 13.7%, down from July — the market calmed a little.

2. Why prices moved

  • Pushed up: NOAA putting the chance of a very strong El Niño above 90%; worry over Brazil's September–October flowering; Arabica stocks falling all month; a magnitude 7.4 earthquake in Colombia; a slow Brazilian harvest.
  • Pushed down: forecasts of a surplus next year (Marex 10.5 million bags, Rabobank 8.9 million); Brazilian warehouses near capacity, pressing farmers to sell sooner; good rain in Brazil at month's end.
  • Neutral this time: the US 25% tariff on Brazil exempts green coffee; the Brazilian real held steady.

3. Stocks

  • Certified Arabica stocks in New York fell to 0.24 million bags (223,976 bags) — the lowest since 1999, roughly 68% below a year earlier.
  • London Robusta stocks rose 19.5% to 0.83 million bags — the highest in nine months.

4. Exports (July 2026)

  • World green bean exports 10.76 million bags, +6.3%. But the coffee-year total so far is 102.87 million bags — 0.4% below last year.
  • Only Robustas rose: +32.0% to 4.98 million bags (Viet Nam +87.4%, Brazil +83.7%).
  • Arabica fell 8.9% to 5.78 million bags. Colombian Milds −11.1%, Brazilian Naturals −10.0%, Other Milds −6.7%.
  • Arabica's share of exports fell from 63.6% to 59.7%.
  • All forms of coffee: 12.23 million bags (+3.3%): Asia & Oceania +17.6%, South America +3.1%, Africa −6.7%, Mexico & Central America −15.9%.

5. Production and consumption (2025/26)

  • World production up 4.4% to 183.6 million bags (Arabica 104.4, Robusta 79.2).
  • Consumption down 0.8% to 180.6 million bags, mainly a drop in the United States.
  • After four deficit years from 2021/22 to 2024/25, the market is expected to run a 3.0 million bag surplus this year.
  • South America grows the most (45.8% of world output); Europe drinks the most.
