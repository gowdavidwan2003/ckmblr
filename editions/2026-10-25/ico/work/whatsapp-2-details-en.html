1. Where prices stood

  • ICO composite (I-CIP) 262.18 US cents/lb — down 8.7% from August. Range for the month 248.44 to 279.85. Closed 30 September at 261.60 — 7.0% below August's close (281.15).
  • Colombian Milds 356.15 (−8.1%), Other Milds 329.44 (−8.8%), Brazilian Naturals 290.19 (−9.9%), Robustas 167.49 (−7.3%). Brazilian Naturals fell the most — the group most directly exposed to Brazil's harvest.
  • New York Arabica futures −10.4% (280.68); London Robusta futures −8.0% (154.28). The gap between the two markets narrowed 13.2% to 126.40.
  • Volatility 10.0%, down 3.7 points from August's 13.7% — the market calmed down again.

2. Why prices moved

  • Pushed down: Brazil's harvest was 97% complete by 26 August; CONAB raised its 2026/27 crop estimate to a record 67.6 million bags. ICO expects 2025/26 consumption to decline 0.8%, including a drop in the United States. The ICO expects a 3.0 million bag surplus this year; USDA puts global 2026/27 output at a record 189.7 million bags. Non-commercial shorts rose through mid-September, with some short covering late in the month (net long up 3,219 contracts to 13,492).
  • Two-sided: NOAA's 10 September call of a greater than 90% chance of a very strong El Niño added a price-premium risk, but September rain supported Brazil's flowering and eased some of that worry. Late-September heat and uncertainty over October rain, plus drought alerts in Honduras and Nicaragua, remain risks. Arabica stocks are still low but have started rebuilding; London Robusta stocks hit a 10-month high.

3. Stocks

  • Certified Arabica stocks in New York fell to 217,646 bags on 15 September — the lowest since 1999. But Olam and Louis Dreyfus certified fresh stock, and the total climbed to 260,654 bags by 30 September.
  • Certified Robusta stocks in London rose to 0.87 million bags — a 10-month high, up from 0.83 million in August.

4. Exports (August 2026)

  • World green bean exports 10.94 million bags, +15.6% (from 9.46 million in August 2025) — bucking the usual July-to-August seasonal dip.
  • Robusta exports rose 22.4% to 4.36 million bags (Viet Nam +69.2%, Brazil +54.9%). Coffee-year-to-date exports up 11.8% to 45.59 million bags.
  • Colombian Milds were the only group to fall: −5.6% to 1.09 million bags. Brazilian Naturals rose 21.3% to 3.57 million bags (Brazil's highest August level on record); Other Milds rose 6.6% to 1.91 million bags.
  • Total Arabica exports rose 11.6% to 6.58 million bags. But Arabica's export share fell below 60% for the first time on record, to 59.6% (from 63.5% a year earlier).
  • All forms of coffee combined: 12.55 million bags (+14.6%): Asia & Oceania +23.1%, South America +20.2%, Africa −1.8%, Caribbean/Mexico/Central America −14.3%.

5. Production and consumption (2025/26)

  • World production up 4.4% to 183.6 million bags (Arabica 104.4, Robusta 79.2).
  • Consumption down 0.8% to 180.6 million bags, mainly a drop in the United States.
  • After four deficit years from 2021/22 to 2024/25, the market is expected to run a 3.0 million bag surplus this year.
  • Robusta's share of world production has risen to 43.1%, up from an average 41.7% over the previous two years.
