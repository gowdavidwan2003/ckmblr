I-CIP drops 8.7% as Brazil’s record crop
reaches the market
Green coffee prices:

The ICO Composite Indicator Price (I-CIP) averaged 262.18 US cents/lb in September 2026, down 8.7% from
August 2026. Increased availability from Brazil’s harvest and expectations of ample supply weighed on prices.
Low certiﬁed Arabica stocks and uncertainty surrounding El Niño provided some support, but all four ICO group
indicators declined.
•        The prices of the Brazilian Naturals, Colombian Milds and Other Milds decreased by 9.9%, 8.1% and 8.8% in
         September 2026 compared with August 2026, averaging 290.19, 356.15 and 329.44 US cents/lb, respectively.
         In the same month, the Robustas declined 7.3% to 167.49 US cents/lb.
•        The arbitrage between the London and New York futures markets narrowed by 13.2% to 126.40 US cents/lb
         in September 2026.
Exports of coffee:
In August 2026, global green bean exports totalled 10.94 million bags, up 15.6% as compared with
9.46 million bags in August 2025. Only the Colombian Milds recorded a downturn, with the following dynamics
in August 2026:
•        Robusta green bean exports increased by 22.4% to 4.36 million bags.
•        Exports of the Colombian Milds decreased by 5.6% to 1.09 million bags.
•        Green bean exports of the Brazilian Naturals increased by 21.3% to 3.57 million bags.
•        Shipments of the Other Milds increased by 6.6% to 1.91 million bags.
Total Arabica exports increased to 6.58 million bags in August 2026, up 11.6% from 5.89 million bags in
August 2025. As a result, the Arabicas’ share of the cumulative green bean exports for the ﬁrst 11 months of
coffee year 2025/26 fell to 59.6% from 63.5% over the same period a year earlier.
Global exports of all forms of coffee increased by 14.6% to 12.55 million bags in August 2026 as compared with
10.95 million bags in August 2025. Two of the four regions recorded falls, with the following dynamics in
August 2026:
•        Exports of all forms of coffee from Asia & Oceania were up 23.1% to 4.03 million bags.
•        Exports of all forms of coffee from Africa decreased by 1.8% to 1.74 million bags.
•        South America’s exports of all forms of coffee increased by 20.2% to 5.98 million bags.
•        Exports of all forms of coffee from the Caribbean, Mexico & Central America decreased by 14.3% to
         0.79 million bags.
In September, the ICO launched the Coffee Market Intelligence Platform (data.ico.org), a free-to-view interactive
tool that combines daily market indicators and data on monthly trade, annual production and consumption. More
information is available in this report.

The Colombian Milds–Other Milds differential increased 2.1%, from 26.15 to 26.71 US cents/lb, between
August and September 2026. The Colombian Milds–Brazilian Naturals differential widened 1.1% to
65.95 US cents/lb, whilst the Colombian Milds–Robustas differential narrowed 8.8% to 188.66 US cents/lb.
The Other Milds–Brazilian Naturals and Other Milds–Robustas differentials moved in opposite directions,
with the former increasing by 0.5% to 39.25 US cents/lb and the latter decreasing 10.4% to
161.96 US cents/lb. The Brazilian Naturals–Robustas differential contracted 13.3% to 122.71 US cents/lb.
The narrowing of every Arabica–Robusta differential reﬂects the larger percentage declines in Arabica
prices as Brazil’s much larger Arabica crop (+34.8%) reached the market, while CONAB’s lower Robusta
estimate (−6.6%) and the Robustas’ early-month resilience cushioned Robusta prices.

The arbitrage between the London and New York futures markets declined by 13.2% to 126.40 US cents/lb
in September 2026.

The volatility of the I-CIP was 10.0% in September 2026, 3.7 percentage points below August’s 13.7%.
Volatility for the Colombian Milds, Other Milds, Brazilian Naturals and Robustas was 10.4%, 11.0%, 12.1%
and 8.8%, respectively; the New York and London futures stood at 13.4% and 9.7%.

Exports of the Colombian Milds decreased by 5.6% to 1.09 million bags in August 2026 from
1.16 million bags in August 2025. This marked the group’s 10th month of negative growth in the current

August’s increase was driven by Brazil, where exports of the Brazilian Naturals were up to 2.92 million bags
in August 2026 from 2.26 million bags a year earlier (Figure VIII). It is the highest August level on record,

Robustas, Brazil’s Brazilian Naturals exports in August 2026 appeared to have beneﬁtted from the fresh
supply available from the 2026/27 crop year harvest, which recently concluded with an estimated

Exports by region – all forms of coffee
Global exports of all forms of coffee increased by 14.6% to 12.55 million bags in August 2026 as compared
with 10.95 million bags in August 2025. This increase brought the year-to-date total to 130.74 million
bags, up 1.0%. The dynamics across the four regions were mixed: exports from Africa and the Caribbean,
Mexico & Central America fell, while shipments from Asia & Oceania and South America increased.
However, South America was the main driver of the August upturn.

Explanatory Note for Table 3


For each year, the Secretariat uses statistics received from Members to provide estimates and
forecasts for annual production, consumption, trade and stocks. As noted in paragraph 100 of
document ICC-120-16, these statistics can be supplemented and complemented by data from
other sources when information received from Members is incomplete, delayed or inconsistent.
The Secretariat also considers multiple sources for generating supply and demand balance sheets
for non-Members.


The Secretariat uses the concept of the marketing year, that is the coffee year commencing on
1 October of each year, when looking at the global supply and demand balance. Coffee-producing
countries are located in different regions around the world, with various crop years, i.e. the 12-
month period from one harvest to the next. The crop years currently used by the Secretariat
commence on 1 April, 1 July and 1 October. To maintain consistency, the Secretariat converts
production data from a crop year basis to a marketing year basis depending on the harvest
months for each country. Using a coffee year basis for the global coffee supply and demand, as
well as prices, ensures that analysis of the market situation occurs within the same time period.


For example, the 2022/23 coffee year began on 1 October 2022 and ended 30 September 2023.
However, for producers with crop years commencing on 1 April, the crop year production occurs
across two coffee years. Brazil’s 2022/23 crop year began on 1 April 2022 and finished 31 March
2023, covering the first half of coffee year 2022/23. However, Brazil’s 2023/24 crop year
commenced 1 April 2023 and ended 31 March 2024, covering the latter half of coffee year
2023/24. In order to bring the crop year production into a single coffee year, the Secretariat
would allocate a portion of the April–March 2022/23 crop year production and a portion of the
April–March 2023/24 production into 2022/23 coffee year production.


It should be noted that while estimates for coffee year production are created for each individual
country, these are made for the purpose of creating a consistent aggregated supply-demand
balance for analytical purposes and do not represent the production occurring on the ground
within the individual countries.
